# Twin Lakes Web Co. Brand Guide

**Legal entity:** Twin Lakes Web Co. LLC  
**Public brand:** Twin Lakes Web Co.  
**Display wordmark:** TWIN LAKES / WEB CO.  
**Primary domain:** twinlakesweb.com

This guide defines the voice, positioning, and visual direction for Twin Lakes Web Co. It replaces the Custom Web Architecture brand guide. Custom Web Architecture and the CWA identity are retired and should not appear in new public-facing materials.

## 1. Brand foundation

### Brand idea

Twin Lakes Web Co. is a local website design and development company rooted in Grayson County, Kentucky. The name refers to the two lakes that define the area:

- Rough River Lake
- Nolin Lake

The brand should feel equally relevant to year-round residents, local businesses, nonprofit organizations, property owners, and people who visit the area to enjoy the lakes.

### Brand personality

Approachable, dependable, skilled, practical, personal, and distinctly local.

The test for every new message or design decision is:

> Does this feel like a capable, trustworthy neighbor who knows the area and happens to be very good at building websites?

### Positioning statement

Twin Lakes Web Co. builds straightforward, professional websites for businesses and organizations in Grayson County and across Central Kentucky. Clients receive personal service, honest guidance, and a website built to perform well without unnecessary complexity.

### Brand promise

Professional web work with local accountability.

### Primary tagline

**Websites built here. Built to work everywhere.**

### Supporting lines

- Local websites. Built right here.
- Websites built with care and common sense.
- Website design and development in Grayson County, Kentucky.
- Serving the communities around Rough River Lake and Nolin Lake.

Use one tagline or supporting line at a time. Do not stack multiple slogans together.

## 2. Audience and geographic positioning

### Primary audience

- Small businesses in Grayson County
- Contractors, restaurants, shops, service providers, and professional offices
- Tourism, lodging, recreation, and lake-area businesses
- Realtors, property managers, and real estate-related services
- Nonprofit, civic, school, church, and community organizations
- Entrepreneurs who want personal help rather than a large agency experience

### Primary service area

- Leitchfield
- Clarkson
- Caneyville
- Rough River Lake communities
- Nolin Lake communities
- Grayson County, Kentucky

### Growth market

The brand may also serve Breckinridge, Hardin, Meade, Bullitt, Larue, and Jefferson counties and clients elsewhere in Kentucky. Local positioning should establish the company's roots without suggesting that work is limited only to Grayson County.

### Residents and visitors

When appropriate, imagery and copy may reference lake life, local commerce, hospitality, recreation, and the practical needs of people who live, work, own property, or spend time in the Twin Lakes area.

Avoid presenting the company as a tourism bureau, lake resort, marina, or government organization. Twin Lakes Web Co. is a web company with a genuine local identity.

## 4. Logo system

### Primary wordmark

The preferred display treatment is:

**TWIN LAKES / WEB CO.**

The slash is a deliberate part of the display identity. It gives the name structure and a subtle digital or development character without relying on cliché code brackets.

### Recommended logo family

Create and maintain these approved versions:

1. **Primary horizontal wordmark:** TWIN LAKES / WEB CO.
2. **Stacked wordmark:** TWIN LAKES above WEB CO.
3. **Compact monogram:** a custom TL mark for square and circular spaces
4. **One-color dark version:** for cream, white, and light backgrounds
5. **One-color light version:** for charcoal, green, photography, and dark backgrounds

### Logo concept direction

The identity may use restrained references to the area's two lakes. Appropriate concepts include:

- Two simple waterlines
- Two parallel contours
- Negative space suggesting two shorelines
- A subtle pairing of forms within a TL monogram

Keep the reference abstract. Do not use detailed lake maps, clip-art waves, fish, boats, anchors, sunsets, or literal landscape badges in the primary logo.

### Clear space

Maintain clear space around the logo equal to at least the height of the capital **T** in the wordmark. No text, border, photograph edge, or other graphic should enter that area.

### Minimum size

- Digital horizontal logo: no smaller than 180 pixels wide
- Print horizontal logo: no smaller than 1.5 inches wide
- Monogram: no smaller than 24 pixels or 0.3 inches

### Logo misuse

Do not:

- Stretch, compress, rotate, outline, or add a drop shadow
- Change individual letters to unapproved colors
- place the logo over a busy image without sufficient contrast
- Replace the slash with a pipe, backslash, or hyphen
- Add “LLC” to the public-facing wordmark
- Combine the new name with any CWA mark

### Legacy assets

The following assets belong to the retired Custom Web Architecture identity and are not approved for new Twin Lakes Web Co. materials:

- `cwa-horizontal-black.png`
- `cwa-monogram-transparent-black.png`
- `cwa-monogram-transparent-white.png`
- Existing CWA favicons and app icons

They may be retained temporarily only for migration reference. Replace them across the website, social profiles, templates, proposals, and client-facing systems after the new Twin Lakes Web Co. logo suite is finalized.

## 5. Color system

The established CWA palette may carry forward during the transition. Its warm orange, practical charcoal, soft cream, and natural green suit the new local identity and avoid predictable blue-wave branding.

| Token | Hex | Primary use |
|---|---|---|
| Twin Lakes Orange | `#F47A3C` | Primary buttons, links, highlights, and calls to action |
| Deep Orange | `#D6602A` | Hover states and darker accents |
| Charcoal | `#242424` | Body text, headlines, and footer backgrounds |
| Warm Cream | `#F6F1E8` | Primary page background |
| Soft Cream | `#FBF8F3` | Header backgrounds and subtle surfaces |
| Lake Country Green | `#64745A` | Secondary accents, metadata, and place-based details |
| Shoreline Gray | `#D9D6D0` | Borders, dividers, and card backgrounds |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Functional contrast on dark backgrounds |

### Color balance

- Cream and soft cream should dominate most layouts.
- Charcoal should carry typography and structural contrast.
- Orange is the primary action color and should be used intentionally.
- Green is a secondary accent, not the dominant brand color.
- Blue is not required merely because the company references lakes.

All text and background combinations must meet WCAG AA contrast standards.

## 6. Typography

### Primary typefaces

- **Headings:** Archivo, weights 600, 700, and 800
- **Body:** Inter, weights 400, 500, 600, and 700

### Typography character

Archivo gives the brand a sturdy, modern, working-company quality. Inter keeps body copy clear and accessible. Together they should feel professional without becoming corporate or overly polished.

### Web implementation

Load fonts efficiently with preconnect and `font-display: swap`. Use a fluid `clamp()` type scale so headings resize smoothly across device widths.

### Type rules

- Use sentence case for most headlines and buttons.
- Reserve all caps for the wordmark, short labels, and small navigation details.
- Do not use decorative script, rustic Western, nautical, or novelty fonts.
- Keep line lengths readable and layouts spacious.

## 7. Voice and language

### Voice

Write plainly, confidently, and personably. The company should sound like Casey speaking directly with a client, backed by a professional company identity.

First-person singular is appropriate when discussing Casey's experience or process:

> I will walk you through the options and explain what each one means.

Use the company name or a neutral construction when describing formal services:

> Twin Lakes Web Co. designs and develops websites for Kentucky businesses.

Do not manufacture a large team by using “we” when Casey is the person providing the service.

### Writing principles

- Explain technical terms in ordinary language.
- Lead with the client's goal, not the technology.
- Be specific about deliverables and expectations.
- Sound helpful without sounding sales-heavy.
- Emphasize personal service, local understanding, and practical results.
- Use contractions where they make the writing sound natural.

### Avoid

- Agency filler such as “we leverage,” “innovative solutions,” or “elevate your brand”
- Unsupported superlatives such as “the best” or “number one”
- Jargon such as CMS, DNS, hosting stack, or conversion architecture without an explanation
- Forced lake puns or excessive nautical language
- Claims that every client is local
- Invented testimonials, statistics, certifications, partnerships, or outcomes
- Emojis in core brand, proposal, and website copy
- Em dashes

### Example

Avoid:

> We leverage cutting-edge digital solutions to elevate your online presence.

Prefer:

> I build fast, professional websites that make it easier for customers to find your business and understand what you offer.

## 8. Messaging pillars

### Local understanding

Twin Lakes Web Co. understands the businesses, communities, and seasonal rhythms around Rough River Lake, Nolin Lake, and Grayson County.

### Personal service

Clients work directly with the person building their website.

### Practical craftsmanship

Websites should be fast, clear, accessible, maintainable, and built for real business needs.

### Honest guidance

Recommendations should fit the client instead of pushing unnecessary features or recurring costs.

### Room to grow

The company can provide website design, development, e-commerce, SEO, Google Search Console setup, hosting and maintenance, domain guidance, custom web applications, and automation.

Whenever SEO optimization is included in a proposal, include Google Search Console setup in that scope.

## 10. Image direction

### Preferred imagery

- Real screenshots of completed client projects
- Real photography of Casey
- Authentic photography of Grayson County businesses and communities
- Restrained images of Rough River Lake and Nolin Lake
- Local streetscapes, storefronts, workspaces, lake access, cabins, lodging, and recreation where relevant
- Simple branded graphics using the approved palette

### Geographic accuracy

When an image is presented as Rough River Lake, Nolin Lake, Leitchfield, Clarkson, Caneyville, or another named place, verify that it actually depicts that location. Do not use generic stock lake photography and label it as a local landmark.

### Personal imagery

The professional headshot may be used on the About page, proposals, speaking materials, and profiles. Personal illustrations such as `drummer.png` may appear sparingly when telling Casey's story, but they are not business marks.

### Avoid

- Generic handshakes and office stock photography
- Fake device mockups that obscure the actual client work
- Police uniforms, badges, patches, vehicles, ranks, or law-enforcement imagery
- Camouflage, tactical graphics, crosshairs, or military styling
- Clip-art lakes, fish, anchors, boats, and compass icons
- Heavy filters that make local photography look artificial

## 11. Digital design system

### Overall look

Warm, clean, capable, and local. Pages should use generous spacing, strong typography, clear hierarchy, and practical navigation. The website should feel modern without chasing design trends.

### Interface components

Maintain shared styles for:

- Primary, secondary, and quiet buttons
- General content and portfolio cards
- Project thumbnails and case studies
- Numbered process steps
- Call-to-action bands
- Forms, field rows, radio groups, and validation states
- Site header, mobile navigation, and footer
- Blog cards, article layouts, and location/service pages

### Accessibility and performance

- Meet WCAG AA contrast requirements.
- Include visible keyboard focus states.
- Use descriptive alternative text.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Optimize photographs and project screenshots.
- Avoid autoplay audio or video.
- Keep navigation and forms usable on small screens.

## 13. Business and product architecture

Twin Lakes Web Co. is the parent service brand. Independent platforms and products may use their own names and identities while crediting the company where appropriate.

Approved form:

> A product developed by Twin Lakes Web Co.

Use the legal name, **Twin Lakes Web Co. LLC**, in contracts, invoices, tax documents, policies, and other formal records.

## 14. Migration checklist

Retire Custom Web Architecture from:

- Website title, navigation, footer, metadata, schema, and Open Graph images
- Domain and professional email references
- Social names, bios, avatars, banners, and link destinations
- Proposals, contracts, invoices, intake forms, and email templates
- Google Business Profile and directory listings
- GitHub organization or repository descriptions where public branding appears
- Favicons, app icons, PWA manifest, and bookmarked icons
- Blog templates, RSS feed, sitemap references, and automated article prompts
- Image alternative text, filenames, and structured data

Use redirects where URLs change. Preserve established website content and search equity rather than deleting useful pages solely because the brand changed.
