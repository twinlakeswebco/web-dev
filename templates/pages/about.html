Two lakes, one county, and a company named for both

Grayson County sits between Rough River Lake and Nolin Lake. Those two lakes shape a lot of life here. They bring visitors in the summer, they give a good number of local businesses their busiest season, and they are the reason people who grew up here tend to come back. Twin Lakes Web Co. is named for them because this is where the company is from and where most of its clients are.

I'm Casey Keown. I build fast, professional websites and practical web applications for small businesses, contractors, shops, nonprofits, churches, and community organizations. The work happens here in Grayson County, and the sites I build are made to work everywhere, on any phone, on any connection, for any customer who finds you.

Why I do this work

I like the craft of it. Figuring out what a business actually needs and then building something that fits is satisfying in a way that generic work never is. A good website should feel like it was made for the business it represents, not pulled off a shelf and repainted.

Most of the owners I talk with have been sold something confusing at least once. A site they can't edit. A monthly fee nobody explained. A page that looks fine on a laptop and falls apart on a phone. I'd rather explain the options in ordinary language, recommend the one that fits, and let you make the call with real information in front of you.

How I work with clients

You work with me. That means clear communication, realistic timelines, and someone who remembers the details of your project from one conversation to the next. My approach comes from a career where details matter: understand the objective, communicate clearly, and finish the job. I build websites alongside a full-time public-service career, and every project gets the same attention and follow-through.

I'll also tell you when you don't need something. If a single well-built page will do what you want, I'm not going to talk you into five. Honest guidance is worth more to both of us than one oversized invoice.

The process

Five steps from first conversation to launch

Here's exactly what working together looks like, so there are no surprises in the middle of the project.

  1. We talk about your business

    What you do, who you serve, what customers ask you most often, and what your website needs to accomplish. No technical questions, and no charge for the conversation.

  2. I put together a practical plan

    You get a written scope in plain language: what I'll build, what it costs, what it costs to keep running, and what is not included. If something in the plan doesn't earn its place, I'll say so.

  3. I design and build the site

    I write and structure the pages, build the design, and get everything working on phones first. You see progress as it happens rather than waiting for one big reveal.

  4. You review it and we refine

    You look at the real site, not a picture of one. We work through your notes together until the details are right.

  5. I launch it and hand you the keys

    Your domain, your hosting, your content, and your accounts stay yours. I'll show you how to make updates, set up Google Search Console when search is in scope, and stay available when you need changes later.
