# Managing a Website Project From First Call to Launch

A website project becomes difficult when decisions are scattered across calls, texts, and several people’s competing instructions.

## The direct answer

Manage a website project with a written scope, named decision-maker, content deadlines, review milestones, and customer-task acceptance checks.

## Set the decision structure

Choose one person who can approve scope, copy, design, and launch. Other staff can contribute, but the designer needs a consolidated answer. Keep a simple decision log with the decision, owner, and date.

Write down the business goal and required customer task. A contractor requesting estimates and a retailer selling products need different definitions of done. Make those definitions visible throughout the project.

## Prepare content as a deliverable

Assign responsibility for service facts, writing, photography, policies, and account access. Give each item a due date. If content is missing, decide whether to delay, reduce scope, or commission help.

Do not approve placeholder text as finished content. A design can be reviewed with samples, but the live site needs accurate service details, hours, territory, and next steps.

## Review in stages

Approve the page plan before detailed design. Approve representative layouts before the entire build. Review function before launch. Each milestone should have a clear output and review window.

Separate defects from new requests. A broken agreed form is a defect. Adding payment collection to a quote form is a scope change. Ask for cost and schedule effects before approving changes.

## Finish with a handoff

Test the main customer paths on a phone, check notifications, and confirm production accounts. Record who handles updates and how problems are reported.

For Kentucky businesses, make sure service-area wording matches actual operations. A statewide design provider can serve remotely without pretending to have offices in every city.

## Keep a short project record

A useful weekly note lists what was approved, what is waiting, who owns the next action, and any scope change. Keep it in one agreed location. It can be brief. The goal is to avoid reconstructing decisions from several text threads. When a decision changes, record the replacement and its schedule effect so everyone works from the same current version.

## Before you decide

- Choose one approver.
- Track content and access.
- Review milestones.
- Keep a written change log.

## What if the project stalls?

Identify the specific dependency and its owner. Agree on a smaller next deliverable instead of repeatedly moving the entire launch date.
