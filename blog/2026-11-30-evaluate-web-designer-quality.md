# How to Tell Whether Your Web Designer Does Good Work

A website can look impressive and still leave customers unable to contact the business.

## The direct answer

Judge a designer by clear reasoning, relevant live work, reliable customer tasks, accurate content, and a usable handoff. Appearance is only part of the assessment.

## Ask for the reasoning

Have the designer explain a page decision in terms of the customer. Why this navigation? Why this form? Why this page structure?

A useful answer connects a choice to audience needs and tradeoffs. Technical vocabulary alone does not establish quality.

## Observe the working site

Review it on a phone, enlarge the text, use the keyboard, and follow service links. Ask for a demo of any function that would create a real order or inquiry.

Check readable content and clear next steps. A fast homepage does not compensate for a confusing checkout or a broken estimate form.

## Look at the project process

Expect written scope, staged reviews, clear feedback handling, and testing evidence. Ask how defects are resolved and how new requirements are priced.

A capable provider should identify uncertainty and ask business questions rather than confidently inventing policies or service claims.

## Evaluate what happens afterward

Can the owner make agreed edits? Are accounts accessible? Is there a documented support route? Can someone else take over?

Those answers reveal whether the website was built as a maintainable business asset. For Kentucky owners, dependable operation matters long after the launch announcement.

## Evaluate a specific explanation

Ask why a quote form requests a project city before a full address. The designer might explain that territory qualification is needed while the exact address is unnecessary for the first conversation. That is a testable privacy and workflow decision. A good answer should connect fields to the office process. Asking for every possible detail by default can create friction without improving the response.

## Before you decide

- Ask why decisions were made.
- Inspect live tasks.
- Review testing evidence.
- Check handoff quality.

## Can I judge quality without knowing code?

Yes. You can evaluate the customer experience, communication, accuracy, and ownership, and ask for a technical review when the project warrants it.
