# Web Designer Red Flags You Can Check Before Paying

Pressure to pay quickly can distract you from the questions that determine whether a website project is safe to start.

## The direct answer

Pause when ownership is unclear, scope stays verbal, results are guaranteed without evidence, or the provider cannot explain delivery and support.

## Vague promises and missing scope

“Unlimited everything” sounds reassuring until you ask who supplies copy, how revisions are handled, or whether booking software is included. Get specific outputs and exclusions.

A refusal to define scope creates a dispute waiting to happen. A provider may need paid discovery before pricing a complex project, but the discovery itself should have a clear deliverable.

## Access controlled entirely by the vendor

Ask whose name and email are on the domain, hosting, CMS, and analytics accounts. If the answer is always the provider, ask for a written handoff and exit process.

[ICANN’s registrant information](https://www.icann.org/registrants) explains domain rights and responsibilities. Keep recovery details with the business rather than depending on a former employee or outside vendor.

## Claims that cannot be checked

Guaranteed first-place rankings, unsupported revenue claims, copied portfolios, and badges with no explanation deserve scrutiny. Ask what the provider built and how results were measured.

A trustworthy proposal can describe technical work and expected goals while acknowledging uncertainty. Search competition and customer demand are not under a designer’s complete control.

## Pressure without a working process

Ask how milestones are reviewed and what happens if either side is delayed. Avoid paying for a large undefined project because a discount expires tonight.

If the provider answers clearly and corrects a missing detail, evaluate the correction. A small administrative mistake is different from a repeated refusal to document responsibilities.

## Verify a ranking claim

If a provider promises to put you first for custom websites in Kentucky, ask which query, location, time period, and measurement method support the promise. A single screenshot cannot establish a durable result for every searcher. Ask instead for the technical work, content plan, reporting, and limitations. A provider can commit to completing those tasks while acknowledging that search engines and competitors affect the result.

## Before you decide

- Request a written scope.
- Verify domain control.
- Review relevant live work.
- Avoid unsupported ranking guarantees.

## Is full upfront payment always a red flag?

No. A small fixed service may reasonably be prepaid. Risk depends on scope, evidence, terms, and the amount exposed.
