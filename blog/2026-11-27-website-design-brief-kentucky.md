# Write a Website Design Brief a Builder Can Quote

A brief should reduce guessing. It does not need to sound technical or contain a finished design.

## The direct answer

A useful website brief describes the business, audience, customer tasks, pages, required features, content responsibilities, budget, timing, and handoff.

## Describe the business and customer

State what you sell, who buys, and where you serve. Include actual service limitations. A statewide consultant and a local repair company need different location language.

Explain the customer’s situation when visiting. Are they requesting an urgent repair, researching a larger project, or buying an item? Name the main action and the questions that come before it.

## List scope and content

Identify the expected pages, integrations, forms, editing tools, and migration needs. Mark each as required or optional.

Assign writing, photos, logo files, policies, and account access. If you do not have materials, say so. Missing content is a project requirement to solve, not an embarrassment to hide.

## Add useful references

Choose a few sites and explain exactly what you like. Point to clear service categories, readable layouts, or useful booking steps. Avoid requesting a copy of another brand.

Include your existing logo and brand constraints. Explain preferences with customer consequences: the current text is hard to read or the phone number is difficult to find.

## Make the quote comparable

State a budget range, desired launch date, decision-maker, and review process. Ask bidders to list assumptions and exclusions.

Request ownership, recurring costs, training, support, and exit terms. Send the same version to each provider so you compare the same project.

## A brief can include uncertainty

Write that you do not yet know whether booking should be confirmed automatically or reviewed by staff. Ask the provider to evaluate the options and explain the added work. That is better than naming a feature without its rules. Separate decisions that discovery must resolve from requirements you already know. A good brief makes uncertainty visible so it can be priced and addressed.

## Before you decide

- Business and territory.
- Audience and main action.
- Pages and functions.
- Content, budget, timing, and handoff.

## How long should the brief be?

Long enough to remove major uncertainty. A clear two-page brief can be more useful than a large collection of unexplained screenshots.
