# What Should a Professional Business Website Include?

A polished homepage can hide an incomplete website. Use customer tasks to judge the finished product before approving launch.

## The direct answer

A professional website should clearly identify the business, explain its services and territory, support the main customer task, work on phones, and have dependable ownership and maintenance arrangements.

## Answer the first five questions

What do you do? Who is it for? Where do you work? Why should someone trust you? What should they do next? Visitors should find those answers without translating vague slogans.

A Kentucky service business should state its genuine coverage. A statewide online service can say so; a contractor with a limited dispatch area should describe it accurately.

## Make contact dependable

Use clear phone links, a usable form, and a confirmation explaining what follows. An appointment request should not imply a confirmed reservation.

[W3C’s forms tutorial](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/) explains labels, instructions, errors, and feedback. Ask the builder to demonstrate a successful submission and a failed one. Both should leave the visitor knowing what to do.

## Provide proof and usable navigation

Show real projects, accurate credentials, permitted reviews, and clear policies where relevant. Keep menus short enough to scan. Name links by their destinations instead of repeating Learn More everywhere.

Use one clear main heading per page and meaningful section headings. Customers should understand a service page even when they arrive directly from search rather than through the homepage.

## Include the behind-the-scenes handoff

Ask for titles and descriptions, canonical URLs, a sitemap, indexability checks, social images, analytics access where used, and a maintenance plan. These tasks should be documented rather than inferred from the appearance.

Keep account recovery with the business. A site that only one outside vendor can access leaves you vulnerable when you need an urgent update.

## Review the site as a new customer

Open a service page without using the homepage first. Can you identify the business, understand whether it serves your location, find credible proof, and take the next step? Then try the page with enlarged text and a phone keyboard. Ask the builder to demonstrate notification delivery rather than accepting a screenshot of a success message. That review connects professional quality to an outcome you can observe.

## Before you decide

- Complete the main customer task on a phone.
- Test form success and errors.
- Check factual claims and permission for images.
- Confirm accounts, backups, and editing instructions.

## Does every business need a blog?

No. A blog is useful when someone can maintain original answers to real customer questions. Service information and contact paths come first.
