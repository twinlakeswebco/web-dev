# Questions to Ask a Web Designer About Your Specific Project

A generic interview can produce polished answers without revealing whether the designer understands your business. Bring one real customer task to the conversation.

## The direct answer

Ask the designer to explain how your customer’s main task will work, what the price includes, what you must supply, and what you receive at handoff.

## Ask for a customer walkthrough

Describe a practical scenario: a homeowner finds your service page, checks your territory, sends photos, and requests a quote. Ask the designer to walk through every step, including the office notification and confirmation.

Listen for questions about your availability, response process, and service limitations. A proposal that jumps straight to colors may leave the actual workflow unresolved.

## Ask what is excluded

Does the fee include writing, image editing, redirect planning, mobile testing, accessibility review, analytics, and third-party setup? Ask for named outputs rather than accepting “everything you need.”

Ask how revisions are counted and how changes outside scope are priced. Replacing a photograph and adding a customer portal are different requests. The agreement should make that distinction understandable before work begins.

## Ask who controls the accounts

Get clear answers about the domain, hosting, source files, CMS, analytics, forms, and paid services. Ask what happens if the designer becomes unavailable or you move to another provider.

Access and ownership are different. You may be able to edit text while the vendor controls the subscription that keeps the site online. Ask which accounts you own and what can be exported.

## Ask how delivery is demonstrated

Request a staging review and a launch checklist. Ask how form delivery, links, phones, keyboard navigation, and important redirects are checked. A live homepage is not evidence that every business function works.

For a redesign, ask how useful existing URLs and search signals will be handled. For a new site, ask how the company will verify crawlability and provide Search Console access.

## A useful question to bring to the interview

Tell the designer: a customer near Leitchfield needs one of our services but does not know its technical name. How will they find the right page and submit useful details? Ask what happens after submission and who receives the request. The answer should cover wording, navigation, information collection, confirmation, and office follow-up. It should also identify where your business must supply decisions rather than inventing them.

## Before you decide

- Bring one real customer scenario.
- Request exclusions in writing.
- Confirm account and file handoff.
- Agree on acceptance checks and support.

## Should I ask about the technology?

Yes, in practical terms. Ask how you edit the site, who maintains it, what subscriptions it requires, and how you leave.
