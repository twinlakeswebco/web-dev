# Hiring Mistakes That Make Website Projects Harder

Many website disputes begin with assumptions both sides thought were obvious.

## The direct answer

Avoid hiring on appearance or price alone, leaving scope verbal, splitting feedback across staff, and ignoring ownership or maintenance until launch.

## Choosing before defining the problem

A request for a modern website can produce wildly different proposals. State the real issue: customers cannot find services, forms fail, staff cannot edit, or the old system cannot support booking.

Evaluate providers against that problem. A dramatic portfolio does not prove experience with your required workflow.

## Comparing incomplete prices

Normalize copy, images, integrations, migration, testing, and handoff before comparing fees. Add recurring services and renewal obligations.

A low quote can be appropriate for limited work. The mistake is assuming the provider included tasks that were never discussed.

## Giving contradictory feedback

Choose one approver and consolidate notes. A designer cannot resolve conflicting business decisions by adjusting the layout.

Make changes through a clear process. Ask how a new requirement affects scope, price, and timing before accepting it.

## Leaving the exit plan until later

Confirm business ownership of the domain and appropriate access to hosting, CMS, analytics, and source files. Ask what a new provider receives if you leave.

For a Kentucky owner balancing business operations and a build, a written handoff is easier to manage than a collection of passwords and explanations scattered across messages.

## Recover from a weak start

If the project has already begun, list what is finished, what remains, which payments were made, and which accounts exist. Ask both sides to approve a revised scope before further work. Save current files and access details. Avoid treating a restart as permission to skip unresolved ownership or content questions. Those issues usually follow the project until someone answers them.

## Before you decide

- Define the problem.
- Compare equivalent scopes.
- Name one approver.
- Document handoff early.

## Can I recover a poorly started project?

Often. Inventory completed work, access, content, and obligations, then agree on a bounded recovery phase before adding more work.
