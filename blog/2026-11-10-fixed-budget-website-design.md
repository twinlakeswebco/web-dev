# How to Hire a Web Designer Within a Fixed Budget

Hiding your budget can produce a proposal you cannot use. A fixed budget gives a capable designer a boundary for choosing what to build first.

## The direct answer

Give the designer your budget and required customer tasks, then ask for a scoped first version with optional work priced separately.

## Choose a business outcome

Write one sentence describing what the first version must accomplish. For example, customers should understand your service area and send a usable estimate request. That is more useful than asking for a modern website.

List the pages and information required for that task. Delay features that do not contribute to it. A contractor may benefit more from clear project examples than an animated homepage.

## Ask for alternatives, not a discount alone

A lower price may come from fewer pages, owner-supplied copy, a standard layout, fewer integrations, or a simpler editing setup. Ask what changes with each option.

Do not quietly remove form testing, readable mobile layouts, or ownership arrangements to hit a number. Those omissions can leave you paying for a site that cannot do its basic job.

## Protect the operating budget

Add recurring domain, hosting, software, and support costs. Include the time you will spend making updates. A low upfront quote with an expensive required service may exceed your budget over a year.

Ask what happens when a subscription ends. Can the site stay online elsewhere? Can content be exported? Will paid functions stop? Compare total commitments as well as the initial invoice.

## Use a phase-two list

Keep a written list of postponed features with the reason for delaying each one. Revisit them after launch using real inquiries and customer feedback.

For a Kentucky seasonal business, a phased build might launch before a busy period with clear hours and requests, then add a larger gallery when new photography is available. The second phase should have its own scope and price.

## Scope a first phase

A lake-area service company might begin with its main service, actual coverage, permitted job photos, and a quote request. It can postpone a searchable project archive until there are enough projects to justify one. Ask the designer to keep the later archive possible without charging for it immediately. This is a scope decision, not a promise that later work will be free or that every platform can expand without constraints.

## Before you decide

- Share the budget early.
- Identify one required customer outcome.
- Price recurring costs.
- Keep optional features separate.

## Will sharing my budget make the quote higher?

A transparent scope lets you evaluate the price. Ask for deliverables and alternatives so you can compare rather than relying on an undisclosed ceiling.
