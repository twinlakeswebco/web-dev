# Custom Website or Website Builder: Test the Fit

The right tool should fit your operation after launch, not only look appealing in a demo.

## The direct answer

Choose a builder when its editing and standard functions fit. Choose custom development when specific content, integration, performance, or workflow requirements justify the added work.

## Test the important tasks

List what customers and staff must do. Try a realistic service update, form submission, booking, product change, and content export where relevant.

A builder can be excellent for standard needs. A custom site can be excellent for unusual requirements. Either can be implemented poorly.

## Price the whole setup

Include subscriptions, apps, hosting, domain renewals, support, and your own update time. Ask which costs increase with usage or additional users.

Use current provider terms. Introductory pricing is not a reliable estimate of long-term ownership.

## Understand the exit

Ask whether content, designs, source code, customer data, and domain can move. Some systems export content without reproducing the layout or function elsewhere.

That may be acceptable if you understand it in advance. The problem is discovering a dependency only when you need to leave.

## Choose for the next reasonable stage

A small Kentucky business does not need to buy a hypothetical enterprise platform on day one. It does need a system that can meet foreseeable needs without losing basic control.

Document the expected editing pattern and integrations. Ask the provider to recommend the simplest dependable fit and explain the alternatives.

## Test a future change

Ask how the platform would handle one foreseeable addition, such as another service category or a second booking location. You do not need to build it immediately. You need to know whether it requires a normal update, another subscription, or substantial redevelopment. Document the tradeoff and choose deliberately rather than purchasing speculative flexibility you may never use.

## Before you decide

- Demonstrate real tasks.
- Compare operating costs.
- Check exports and portability.
- Match foreseeable needs.

## Does custom mean I cannot edit it?

No. Custom sites can include content management. Ask for the exact editing experience rather than relying on a label.
