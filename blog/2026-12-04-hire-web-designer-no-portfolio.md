# Hiring a Web Designer With No Portfolio

A new designer may be capable, but you should not have to risk an undefined project to find out.

## The direct answer

A missing portfolio increases uncertainty. Reduce it with a relevant demo, verified contribution to other work, a bounded paid first phase, and clear acceptance criteria.

## Find out what evidence exists

Ask about training projects, personal sites, team contributions, and demonstrations. A designer may be unable to show a private client system but can still explain their role without disclosing confidential information.

Do not mistake confidence for evidence. Ask for a walkthrough of the customer task your business needs, including errors and mobile behavior.

## Use a small paid phase

A discovery brief, representative page, or functioning prototype can establish whether the relationship works. Define the output, fee, schedule, and rights before beginning.

Make the result useful if you stop there. A paid planning document should not depend on continuing with the same provider to understand the recommendations.

## Limit exposure

Avoid paying for a large undefined build. Tie later commitments to reviewed deliverables and keep company accounts under your control.

A lower fee may reflect experience, scope, or a deliberate introductory offer. It does not eliminate the need for testing, ownership, and a support plan.

## Judge communication and judgment

Notice whether the designer asks business questions, admits uncertainty, and explains tradeoffs. Ask how they get specialist help when a requirement exceeds their experience.

For a Kentucky owner, a smaller first project can be a fair way to work with an emerging provider while protecting the business.

## Use a bounded trial responsibly

A paid prototype could include one service page and a working request path. Define mobile behavior, notification delivery, and access as acceptance checks. Use real business facts and authorized assets, but do not expose sensitive customer data. If the trial succeeds, scope the remaining build separately. If it does not, retain the agreed outputs and end the trial according to its terms.

## Before you decide

- Request a relevant demonstration.
- Define a paid first phase.
- Control accounts.
- Review before expanding scope.

## Should I insist on free sample work?

No. Ask for existing evidence or agree on a small paid deliverable with clear terms.
