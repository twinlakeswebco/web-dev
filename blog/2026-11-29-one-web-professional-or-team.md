# One Web Professional or a Design and Development Team?

Team size should explain how the work gets done, not function as a substitute for evidence.

## The direct answer

One capable professional can deliver a straightforward site. A team becomes useful when the project needs several specialties, substantial capacity, or ongoing operational coverage.

## List the skills required

Writing, design, development, photography, search setup, accessibility, integrations, and project management may all appear in one project. Ask who handles each task.

A solo provider may use specialists for particular work. An agency may outsource development. Neither arrangement is automatically wrong when roles and accountability are clear.

## Match capacity to complexity

A small service website may benefit from direct communication with one builder. A large catalog, migration, or application can need parallel specialties and more testing capacity.

Ask about availability, dependencies, and support coverage. The schedule should reflect actual capacity rather than a promise made to win the project.

## Understand coordination costs

Separate designers and developers need shared requirements, design states, and clear review ownership. Someone must resolve differences between a mockup and the working implementation.

Ask who owns the finished customer experience. “The other person handled that” is not a satisfactory handoff when an agreed task fails.

## Judge the proposed process

Request a walkthrough of a comparable build. Review deliverables, communication, support, and account ownership.

For a Kentucky small business, the best setup is one that provides the necessary skills without unnecessary management overhead. Choose the proposal that makes responsibility understandable.

## Compare availability and backup

Ask who handles a critical problem when the main contact is unavailable. A solo provider may have a documented backup arrangement. An agency may assign a support queue with a defined response process. Both answers need specifics. Decide whether that coverage fits the business’s operational dependence on the site, especially if it accepts payments or controls customer bookings.

## Before you decide

- Map skills to people.
- Check capacity.
- Name final accountability.
- Compare support and handoff.

## Does an agency always produce better work?

No. Quality depends on the assigned people, process, scope, and testing, not the size of the company.
