Website Designer vs Developer: Who Does What?

A designer usually plans the visual experience and interactions. A developer builds and connects the functioning site. One person can do both, but.

Published November 14, 2026 · Casey Keown

You ask for a website and receive proposals from designers, developers, studios, and agencies. The job titles do not tell you who will write the copy or make the booking system work.

The direct answer

A designer usually plans the visual experience and interactions. A developer builds and connects the functioning site. One person can do both, but the proposal should identify responsibility for every required task.

Map roles to deliverables

Design can include page structure, typography, color, responsive layouts, and how visitors move through a task. Development turns those decisions into pages, components, integrations, and editing tools. The boundary varies by provider.

Writing, photography, search planning, accessibility, and project management may be separate responsibilities. Ask who handles each one. A complete website requires decisions across all of them even if one person delivers the work.

Choose for the difficult part

If your challenge is explaining services and establishing trust, prioritize content and design judgment. If you need accounts, payments, permissions, or unusual workflows, verify development and testing experience.

For a Kentucky service company, a designer-developer may handle the whole site efficiently. A complex application may justify specialists. The right team size follows the work.

Prevent a handoff gap

A design file is not a live website. A coded page is not necessarily a usable finished product. Confirm whether the contract ends at mockups, a working staging site, or a tested production launch.

Ask how changes are coordinated when design and development are separate. Someone must own the final result, including mobile behavior, error states, and the office’s ability to maintain content.

Map a booking project to people

For a booking website, someone must define the business rules, design availability screens, connect the service, write confirmation wording, test errors, and train staff. Ask who owns each responsibility. A designer-developer can cover several roles, while a booking provider handles others. The company still supplies actual staff hours, prices, cancellation rules, and approval decisions. Naming roles prevents gaps between visual design and operations.

Before you decide

  • List all roles, including writing.
  • Verify the hardest function.
  • Define the final deliverable.
  • Name the person responsible for handoff.

Can one person do both jobs well?

Yes. Judge their relevant work and process rather than assuming a title proves or limits capability.
