# What to Put in Writing Before Hiring a Web Designer

A friendly conversation can begin a project. It should not be the only record of what both sides agreed to do.

## The direct answer

Record scope, deliverables, fees, payment milestones, content duties, revisions, acceptance, ownership, recurring services, and exit terms before work starts.

## Describe the actual work

Name pages, functions, integrations, content work, migration, testing, and launch responsibilities. List exclusions and optional items.

Define what completed means. An online form may need to validate input, deliver a notification, display a confirmation, and handle errors. “Contact page included” does not settle those details.

## Document money and changes

Set the fee, payment triggers, required subscriptions, taxes where applicable, and how additional work is approved. Define revision rounds and who consolidates feedback.

Ask what happens when either party delays, pauses, or ends the project. A clear business agreement reduces uncertainty; obtain qualified advice for legal questions about your specific terms.

## Explain ownership and access

Identify what you receive, when rights transfer, and which third-party licenses remain restricted. Include source, content, designs, photography permissions, domain, and platform accounts.

A vendor cannot transfer rights it does not own. Stock media, fonts, software, and outside services may have separate terms.

## Define support and handoff

Record the defect-reporting period, support route, maintenance scope, training, and exit assistance. Clarify whether future updates are included or separately quoted.

Keep the signed scope and approved changes together. Kentucky business owners should be able to understand the commercial terms without reconstructing months of texts.

## Make acceptance concrete

Write that a form must validate required fields, deliver one notification to the agreed destination, display a confirmation, and provide a usable error if submission fails. Include supported file types if uploads are required. That is clearer than contact form included. Use similar descriptions for booking, checkout, migrations, and editing tasks so the agreement describes observable behavior.

## Before you decide

- Scope and exclusions.
- Fees and milestones.
- Revisions and change approval.
- Ownership, support, and exit.

## Is an email enough?

Written records help, but the enforceability and suitability of a particular agreement are legal questions. Use a clear agreement and seek advice when needed.
