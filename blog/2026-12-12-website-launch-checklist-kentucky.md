# A Website Launch Checklist for Kentucky Businesses

Launch day should be a controlled change, not the first time anyone tests the contact form.

## The direct answer

Launch after the production site’s content, customer tasks, domain, redirects, indexing settings, analytics, social previews, and account ownership have been checked.

## Check business information

Approve service details, territory, hours, phone numbers, staff, policies, and current offers. Remove placeholders and unsupported claims. Verify image permissions.

Read each important landing page on its own. Customers may arrive directly from search without seeing your homepage.

## Test the customer path

Call the phone link, submit a form, check notifications, and review the confirmation. Use approved test modes for purchases and bookings.

Test error states and mobile inputs. A site that handles only the happy path can still fail ordinary customers.

## Protect technical continuity

Confirm HTTPS, intended www or non-www canonical, important redirects, a sitemap, robots settings, and removal of staging noindex tags. Preserve business email records when changing DNS.

Use [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console/about) to inspect discovery and indexing after launch. A submitted sitemap is not a promise that every URL will be indexed.

## Check sharing and ownership

Verify the article or page’s title, description, and social image, including the absolute image URL. Confirm analytics access and named responsibility for maintenance.

Record the launch date and changes. That gives a Kentucky owner a useful baseline when evaluating inquiries and search performance afterward.

## Rehearse a domain change

Record the existing website and email records before changing DNS. Confirm which service will answer the domain, which host is canonical, and how old URLs redirect. Test business email and the live request path after the change. A launch should not accidentally disconnect the inbox that receives customer inquiries. Keep the former setup available for a planned rollback where practical.

## Before you decide

- Approve content.
- Test customer tasks.
- Check DNS and indexing.
- Verify social previews and handoff.

## Should I announce immediately?

Announce once production checks pass. Test the live URL first so the first visitor does not discover a broken workflow.
