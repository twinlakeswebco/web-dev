# Can You Get a Useful Business Website Under $500?

A tiny budget is workable when the job is tiny. Problems begin when a small-site price is expected to cover a full sales, booking, and content system.

## The direct answer

A budget below $500 can support a small, tightly defined website or link page. It usually requires limits on pages, content work, integrations, and ongoing service.

## Choose one useful job

A link page can organize contact details, services, booking links, and social profiles. A small landing page can explain a single offer and provide a dependable next step.

As of November 4, 2026, my [contact page](/contact/) lists The Dock custom link page starting at $200. That is a specific product and scope, not a promise that any business website can be built for that price.

## Prepare your own materials

Supply an approved logo, concise service description, business details, usable photos, and existing account access. Ask which content tasks are included in the quote.

Original writing, new photography, complicated migrations, and custom booking rules can require more work than a limited budget supports. Do not assume a low price makes those tasks disappear.

## Keep essential reliability

The page still needs readable text, mobile usability, working links, a clear contact path, and accurate information. If it uses a form, test delivery and confirmation.

Avoid collecting sensitive information through an improvised setup. Link to an appropriate established provider when the business needs payments or secure applications.

## Plan an upgrade path

Keep the domain under business control and clarify whether the page can expand or move. List hosting and other recurring costs.

A small site can be a sensible first step for a Kentucky startup testing demand. When services and inquiries grow, expand deliberately instead of stacking unmaintained tools onto the original page.

## Know what a small page cannot do

A link page can send visitors to an established booking tool and show reliable contact information. It does not necessarily provide detailed service pages, a large searchable catalog, or a custom scheduling system. Choose it when organizing existing destinations solves the immediate problem. If search visibility for several services is the main goal, ask how the small page fits a longer-term content and website plan.

## Before you decide

- Choose a narrow scope.
- Supply approved content.
- Confirm recurring costs.
- Retain domain control.

## Is a cheap site always bad for SEO?

No. A useful small site can be crawlable and well organized. Limited content may simply answer fewer customer questions.
