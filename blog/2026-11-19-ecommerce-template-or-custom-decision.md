# Online Store Template or Custom Development?

A custom-looking storefront and a custom-built commerce system are different purchases. You may need the first without needing the second.

## The direct answer

Use an established store platform and template when standard catalog, checkout, and fulfillment workflows fit. Add custom development for a documented business requirement the standard tools cannot meet.

## Map the order from product to delivery

List product options, inventory rules, tax configuration, pickup, shipping, notifications, returns, and refunds. Test the unusual cases before approving the platform.

For example, a Kentucky shop offering local pickup and nationwide shipping needs clear pickup eligibility and separate fulfillment messages. A theme screenshot cannot demonstrate those rules.

## Use standard infrastructure where it fits

Established checkout and payment services can reduce the amount of custom infrastructure you must operate. You still need to configure products, permissions, policies, and notifications correctly.

Ask what the monthly platform, app, payment-processing, and support costs are. Use current provider terms rather than a remembered promotional price.

## Custom-build the gap, not the whole world

If your business needs unusual quoting, personalization, wholesale permissions, or production files, document the exact gap. Compare an app, integration, or small custom extension before commissioning an entire commerce engine.

Specify what happens when an external service is unavailable and how orders are recovered. A successful payment must not disappear because an integration failed.

## Test with realistic products

Use products with variants, out-of-stock states, pickup restrictions, and shipping differences. Confirm totals, notifications, refunds, and staff access in approved test modes.

Assign ongoing responsibility for product data and platform updates. The owner must be able to operate the store after the designer leaves.

## A pickup-versus-shipping test

Use one product available for shipping and another restricted to pickup. Check that customers see the correct options and staff receive useful instructions. Then test a failed payment and an unavailable item in approved test mode. A polished template can still need substantial configuration to handle those rules. Document which tool owns inventory and how changes reach the website before approving custom development.

## Before you decide

- Map fulfillment rules.
- Compare total subscriptions.
- Document custom requirements.
- Test failure and refund paths.

## Can a template store still have distinctive branding?

Yes. Original copy, photography, typography, layout choices, and thoughtful product organization can create a distinct experience.
