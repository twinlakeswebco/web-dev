# Compare Website Quotes With the Same Scope

Three prices are not three comparable offers until you know what each one buys.

## The direct answer

Normalize pages, content, functions, ownership, recurring fees, support, and acceptance checks before comparing website quotes. Ask providers to explain gaps in writing.

## Build a comparison sheet

Use one row for each required page, customer action, content task, integration, testing task, and handoff item. Mark included, excluded, optional, or unclear.

Send unclear items back to each provider. Do not fill gaps with optimistic assumptions.

## Separate initial and ongoing totals

Add setup, domain, hosting, software, maintenance, and support. Compare both the first year and a normal renewal year.

A managed plan may include meaningful work, while another quote may require you to handle that work. Compare responsibility alongside price.

## Compare risk and evidence

Look at relevant live work, migration experience, communication, and testing. Ask who will build the project and how defects are handled.

Treat guaranteed rankings and unsupported revenue claims cautiously. A quote should describe work the provider can deliver.

## Resolve tradeoffs explicitly

If a lower quote has a smaller scope that meets your needs, it may be the better purchase. If a higher quote covers difficult integrations or content you cannot provide, the difference may be justified.

For Kentucky owners, local meetings, photography, and service-area research should be priced and described rather than presumed.

## Resolve an unclear quote line

If a proposal lists SEO as a single line, ask which pages receive research, writing, titles, descriptions, internal links, redirects, and indexing checks. If it lists maintenance, ask which updates and response expectations are included. Mark the answer beside the line item. That gives you a practical comparison without guessing the value of a label.

## Before you decide

- Normalize deliverables.
- Calculate recurring costs.
- Resolve unclear items.
- Compare responsibility and evidence.

## How many quotes should I request?

Enough to understand the options without creating a large unpaid bidding exercise. A clear brief improves comparison more than a high quote count.
